## [Claude Fable Finds Counterexample to Jacobian Conjecture](https://xcancel.com/__alpoge__/status/2079028340955197566) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Anthropic employee Levent Alpöge used Claude Fable 5 to discover an explicit counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture in three-dimensional space, disproving the conjecture for N > 2. The result was posted on X (formerly Twitter) rather than in a traditional journal. This marks the first time an AI has solved a major open problem in pure mathematics, potentially transforming how mathematical research is conducted. It also challenges the traditional academic publishing system, as the result was shared directly on social media. The counterexample involves polynomials of degree 7, far lower than previous estimates of around 200. The Jacobian Conjecture remains open for the two-variable case (N=2).

hackernews · loubbrad · Jul 20, 02:51 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48973869)

**Background**: The Jacobian Conjecture, dating back to 1884, asserts that a polynomial map with a non-zero constant Jacobian determinant must have a polynomial inverse. It is number 16 on Stephen Smale's list of problems for the 21st century and has resisted proof for over a century, with many flawed attempts.

## [SSAO Critiqued: Corners Don't Look Like That](https://nothings.org/gamedev/ssao/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A 2012 article by Sean Barrett argues that screenspace ambient occlusion (SSAO) produces unrealistic corner shadows, challenging the technique's widespread use in game rendering. This critique sparked debate on whether SSAO's visual artifacts matter for game graphics, influencing how developers balance realism and performance. The article uses real-world photographs to show that SSAO often darkens corners incorrectly, while community comments note that SSAO was never meant to simulate true ambient occlusion but to improve visual appeal cheaply.

hackernews · firephox · Jul 20, 15:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48979931)

**Background**: Ambient occlusion (AO) is a shading technique that approximates how ambient light is blocked by nearby geometry, creating soft shadows in crevices. SSAO is a real-time approximation that uses the depth buffer to estimate occlusion, first popularized by Crysis in 2007. It became a staple in games due to its low cost, but its accuracy is limited compared to ray-traced AO.
